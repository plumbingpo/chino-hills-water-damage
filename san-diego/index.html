Simple Ways to Keep a San Diego Home Dry

A flood in the house feels like it came from nowhere, yet most trace back to wear that built up over months or years. For homeowners in San Diego, prevention is mainly a matter of knowing where to look.

Common Water Damage Issues in San Diego

Most water damage calls we take in San Diego trace back to a short list of causes. If you can spot these early, you have a good chance of stopping the damage before it spreads.

Pipes: When water freezes inside a line it can crack the metal or plastic, then pour out as soon as things warm up. Depending on the season, the plumbing most at risk runs through outside walls, attics and garages without heat. In older homes, aging joints and tired connectors sometimes fail with no cold snap involved. Shut off the main valve right away, because an open line can soak several rooms quickly.

Appliances: The thin hoses feeding a washer, dishwasher or ice maker do not last forever. A water heater near the end of its life can leak from the tank bottom or fail all at once. We see many of these in San Diego homes where the unit sits upstairs or over a finished room.

Overflows: It takes only a few minutes for a blocked toilet or a forgotten faucet to flood a bathroom. From there it slips beneath the trim and can drip into whatever sits under that floor. Water that backed up out of a drain or toilet bowl is not clean, so keep kids and pets out of it.

Roof: Missing shingles, cracked flashing and clogged gutters let rain into the attic. The stain on your ceiling usually shows up well after the insulation above it is soaked. The crew from Chino Hills Restoration can dry the attic and ceiling, but the roof itself needs a repair to stop the leak.

Basement: Once the ground outside is soaked, water finds its way into a basement through wall cracks and the seam where floor meets wall. A sump pump that loses power or jams during a heavy rain lets the pit overflow. Carpet, wall studs and drywall wick moisture upward, which is why finished lower levels take the worst of it.

Mold: Mold can start growing within 24 to 48 hours on materials that stay wet. Many mold jobs we handle in San Diego began with an old leak that was wiped up but never dried inside the wall. Have it looked at if you notice a stale odor, bubbling paint or dark specks around an earlier leak.
